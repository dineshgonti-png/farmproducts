We grow two things, and only two things

FarmProducts began in 2018 as a turmeric-drying shed in Erode. Today it's a farmer producer company of 340 growers across four states — still selling exactly two crops.

The reason

Narrow beats broad when the thing you're selling is trust

Most organic marketplaces list ten thousand SKUs and know almost nothing about any of them. We went the other way. Two crops means we know every field, every drying rack and every mill run by name.

It also means we can afford to test properly. A curcumin assay costs more than most brands are willing to spend per batch — but if the number isn't on the label, you're buying colour, not compound.

The growers

Four collectives, one supply chain

🫚
Jaintia Hills, MeghalayaJaintia Hills Co-op

Ninety-two families growing Lakadong at 1,400 m. Terraced slopes, forest-leaf mulch, zero chemical input for four generations.

🌽
Malwa Plateau, Madhya PradeshMalwa Corn Growers

140 farmers who moved back to open-pollinated seed in 2019 so they could save and replant instead of buying hybrids each season.

🌿
Erode, Tamil NaduErode Spice Farms

Where we started. Sixty acres on drip irrigation, neem-cake compost and a boiling-and-drying yard we rebuilt in 2021.

🟣
Bastar & DeccanHeritage Seed Partners

Tribal collectives conserving black turmeric and blue corn — landraces that nearly disappeared when hybrids arrived.

🌾
Indore, Madhya PradeshMalwa Stone Mill

Our corn mill. Granite stones turning slowly enough to stay cool, so makki atta keeps its oils instead of scorching them off.

🍵
CoimbatoreFarmProducts Kitchen

Our own small production unit for latte mixes, turmeric pickle and roasted corn snacks. Eleven staff, all women, all on the payroll.
